Shootr Product Audit

Mobile app critique board.

A screenshot-based review of the current Shootr app experience. The product has a strong mobile-first shell, but the flow needs stronger continuity, truthful marketplace states, and production-safe trust patterns.

Captured: July 24, 2026 Viewport: 390 x 844 Mode: Prototype audit No code changes

Highest-impact findings

Trust risk Demo data makes the network feel fake.

“Demo Go Shootr,” fake ratings, demo Moments, and empty Bookings tell conflicting stories. Production mode needs either real data or honest empty states.

Flow risk The intended step order exists, but the lived flow can skip context.

From the normal Book entry, the Now action jumped to “Choose your Shootr,” bypassing Location in the observed walk.

UX risk Bottom navigation overlaps key content.

Several screens put fields or next content under the fixed bottom nav. Add safe-area/scroll padding across app routes.

Strength The brand system is working.

The home screen, typography, Focus Mark, and blue action language feel distinct, simple, and mobile-native.

Flow screenshots and notes

1. Home

Health: Strong
  • “Ready.” is memorable and action-first.
  • Now/Later is the cleanest expression of the product.
  • The value line is slightly cramped on small width.
Keep this screen nearly this simple.

2. Location

Health: Medium
  • The location-denied fallback is useful and direct.
  • Lower location-type controls are hidden by the bottom nav.
  • “Choose on Map” appears before a real map integration exists.
Add safe-area padding and validate map/address behavior.

3. Now or Later

Health: Medium
  • The segmented choice is easy to understand.
  • Date and time fields remain visible when Now is selected, which creates hesitation.
  • No “continue” affordance is visible, so progression can feel mysterious.
Hide date/time for Now or clarify they apply only to Later.

4. Choose your Shootr

Health: Strong
  • Phone, Pro Camera, No Preference is the right mental model.
  • Cards feel tappable and readable.
  • Selected Focus Mark is subtle but understandable.
Keep this interaction. It is one of the best screens.

5. Duration

Health: Good
  • Quick, Standard, Extended is easy.
  • Starting prices are clear enough for prototype mode.
  • Production needs full total before authorization.
Tie duration to a real quote snapshot later.

6. Finding your Shootr

Health: Medium
  • The screen communicates availability and urgency well.
  • “Finding” conflicts with asking the user to tap “See nearby Shootrs.”
  • Progress bar says near-complete, but the system has not really matched anyone.
Either auto-search or rename this to “Search nearby.”

7. Nearby Shootrs

Health: Weak
  • The card layout is strong and scannable.
  • “Demo Go Shootr” immediately damages trust.
  • 4.9 rating should not appear unless backed by real completed bookings.
Remove fake marketplace proof from normal mode.

8. Review

Health: Medium
  • Itemized pricing is the right trust move.
  • Confirm action appears below the fold.
  • Demo Shootr and approximate area still make this feel simulated.
Show total and primary confirm action within first viewport.

9. Bookings

Health: Weak
  • The tabs/categories are understandable.
  • Empty bookings conflict with demo tracker and Moments data elsewhere.
  • No “Book a shoot” recovery action is visible in the empty state.
Add honest empty-state CTA and align demo content globally.

10. Tracker

Health: Strong visual, weak state
  • The map visual is clean and brand-native.
  • “Finding someone” conflicts with showing a marker, route, and 8 min ETA.
  • Needs explicit fallback for unavailable location.
Map UI states to actual booking-state machine states.

11. Moments

Health: Medium
  • Simple private-gallery card is a good direction.
  • Demo Jasmine gallery conflicts with empty Bookings.
  • Expiration is present, which is a good privacy cue.
Only show Moments tied to real completed bookings.

12. Profile

Health: Good
  • Unified account structure is clear.
  • Account rows are familiar and readable.
  • Bottom nav overlaps the next section.
Increase page bottom padding across profile subroutes.

13. Become a Shootr

Health: Good start
  • The legal/private name explanation is reassuring.
  • DOB and age confirmation are appropriate for marketplace safety.
  • Needs clearer save/resume and privacy policy affordance.
Connect this to real application records and resumable progress.

14. Admin

Health: Risk
  • Restricted messaging is better than exposing admin tools.
  • The route is still public and visually reachable.
  • Consumer-style header/CTA makes internal access feel informal.
Move admin behind real internal auth and separate navigation.
